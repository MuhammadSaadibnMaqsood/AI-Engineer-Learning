Covariance vs Correlation (Complete Notes)
Introduction

In statistics and Machine Learning, Covariance and Correlation are two important measures used to understand the relationship between two variables.

For example:

Hours Studied → Exam Score
Height → Weight
Advertising Cost → Sales
Temperature → Ice Cream Sales

Both tell us whether two variables move together, but they differ in how they measure that relationship.

Disadvantages of Covariance

The biggest limitation:

It does not tell the strength of the relationship.

Example:

Dataset A:

Covariance = 120

Dataset B:

Covariance = 5

Can we say Dataset A has a stronger relationship?

No.

Why?

Because covariance depends on the units of measurement.

If we measure height in centimeters instead of meters, covariance changes.

Therefore, covariance values cannot be compared across datasets.

Covariance tells us which direction variables move, while correlation tells us which direction and how strongly they move.

Applications in Machine Learning
1. Feature Selection

Highly correlated features often provide redundant information.

Example:

Age
Years Since Birth

Keeping both may not improve the model.

2. Multicollinearity Detection

In regression models, highly correlated independent variables can lead to unstable coefficient estimates and reduced interpretability.

3. Data Analysis

Understanding relationships between variables before training a model.

4. Financial Modeling

Covariance and correlation help analyze how stocks move together for portfolio diversification.

5. Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

PCA uses the covariance matrix (or correlation matrix after standardization) to identify directions of maximum variance and reduce dimensionality.

Common Interview Questions
Can covariance be greater than 1?
Yes. Covariance has no fixed range.
Can correlation be greater than 1?
No. Correlation is always between −1 and +1.
Why is correlation preferred over covariance?
Because it is standardized, unitless, and allows meaningful comparisons across datasets.
Does zero correlation imply independence?
Not necessarily. Zero correlation only indicates no linear relationship. Variables may still have a nonlinear relationship.
What does a correlation of −0.85 mean?
It indicates a strong negative linear relationship: as one variable increases, the other tends to decrease.
